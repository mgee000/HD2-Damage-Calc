How this is calculated

Armour and durability. Armour penetration is checked first: if the weapon's AP is below the part's armour value the shot bounces for zero. Equal AP and AV deals 65%; higher AP deals full damage. Damage is then split by the part's durability — standard × (1 − durable%) + durable damage × durable% — and each hit also feeds a percentage through to the main health pool. The target dies when a fatal part is destroyed or main health hits zero.

Pellets. Each pellet resolves as its own hit against the part, so a shotgun's trigger pull is simply that many separate impacts — which is why the per-pellet damage, not the advertised total, is what gets checked against armour. The slider defaults to every pellet landing; drag it down to model realistic spread at range. Pellet counts are the figures the community most consistently reports rather than datamined values, and they shift between patches, so treat them as a starting point.

Impact, blast and shrapnel. A round that explodes lands more than one thing on the target, and each one carries its own armour penetration. They are resolved separately and in order — impact, then blast, then fragments — so a shot can punch through with one component and bounce with another. An Ultimatum into a Hulk's head lands the AP6 impact and the AP6 blast; an Eruptor into that same head lands its AP4 impact while the AP3 blast and shrapnel ricochet, which is why the two feel so different despite similar paper numbers. The totals shown are for one trigger pull.

The shrapnel slider is an assumption, not a source. Per-fragment damage comes from the wiki, but how many fragments actually strike your target does not — it depends on range, angle and hitbox, and the fragment count has been changed and removed and re-added across patches. The slider starts at zero so the headline number is the part you can rely on; drag it up to see the best case.

Range and falloff. The wiki's rule is that damage tracks velocity: a round travelling slower than its muzzle speed has lost damage, and one travelling faster — fired while diving, or from a moving FRV — has gained some. Drag is what sheds that velocity and mass is what resists it, so a weapon with a 0% drag factor genuinely loses nothing at any distance while everything else falls away steadily. Each weapon's page carries the three numbers this needs: mass, muzzle velocity and drag factor. Falloff applies to the projectile only — a blast and its fragments fade from where the round detonates, which is a separate system and is not modelled here.

Fall-off is estimated. Fifty-one weapons here carry real range-test data: each one shot at eight fixed distances out to 200m and recorded to within about 0.3%, from the community-compiled Helldivers II Index.

What the numbers actually show. The spread is far wider than any single rule would suggest. An Anti-Materiel Rifle still has 88% of its damage at 200m; a Redeemer is down to 64% at 50m and a Breaker to 76%. Plasma splits in two: the Scorcher is one of the worst in the game at 67.7% lost by 100m, while the Accelerator Rifle beside it in the menu is one of the best. Losses top out near 74.2%, past which the dataset notes the round simply cannot reach. And one weapon runs backwards — the Ultimatum's round is slow enough that it gains damage as it falls under gravity, measured at 26.6% above its listed damage by 50m.

Flat lines, and which kind they are. Thirty-eight weapons are flat because the mechanic genuinely doesn't reach them, and the chart says which reason applies. Beams and sprays fire no discrete projectile, so there is no velocity to shed. Arcs carry no projectile data at all. Guided rounds accelerate back to their initial velocity. Two weapons are measured at 0% drag on their own wiki pages. And explosion rows are flat because the wiki states outright that fall-off applies to the projectile only — a blast does fade, but from where it detonates rather than from where you stood, which is a different system and not modelled here. Only two weapons are flat for want of evidence: the R-4 Hyena and the SG-88 Break-Action are absent from the measured dataset, and their charts say so.

Where these figures come from and what that's worth. The range tests are community measurements, not published by the game or the wiki, which describes fall-off only qualitatively and never prints a rate. They are hand-tested and internally consistent — the projectile stats beside them line up with the mechanic the wiki does describe, with calibre driving loss hardest and mass resisting it — but they are one dataset, and a patch can move them. Treat them as the best available evidence rather than as official values.

Penetration slowdown is not a range stat. It appears next to drag on the same table and is easy to read as one, but it is the velocity a round loses punching through a body: the Liberator's 25% means a round that cleaves a target exits at three quarters of its speed, carrying less damage into whatever is behind it. It costs nothing over open distance. Cleaving itself — which needs the round's AP to exceed the part's armour by 2 or more, and drops its AP by 1 per hitbox — is not modelled here at all, so penetration slowdown does not enter the arithmetic.

Burning. Fire is a status rather than part of the round. Each impact adds its buildup to a meter, and once that meter passes the target's ignition threshold the target catches and burns for a fixed time no matter what you do next. Burning deals 100 a second at AP4, does the same damage to durable parts as to soft ones, and lasts three seconds unmodified — all four figures are the wiki's. Each enemy then scales it by its own fire multiplier, and the burn ticks against main health only, so it does nothing to speed up a kill that depends on destroying a fatal part. If the target dies at or before the shot that guarantees ignition, the burn is not counted at all.

Which fire numbers are sourced and which are not. The 100/s, the AP4, the three second duration and the multipliers for the Warrior, Brood Commander, Charger, Impaler, Bile Titan and Hulk all come from the wiki, as does the Hulk's minimum ignition threshold of 9 and the Breaker Incendiary's 0.6 buildup per pellet. Every other ignition threshold and every other weapon's buildup per hit is an estimate — those values are not published anywhere. That is why buildup per hit is an editable box rather than a fixed figure: change it if you have better numbers. Treat the shots-to-ignite figure as a ballpark, not a measurement.

Units. Beam and flame weapons are listed by damage per second or per tick, so their result reads as a duration on target rather than a count of trigger pulls — the label under the number tells you which. The Meltagun is the exception: it fires in discrete charged bursts, so it is counted in shots like any other weapon.

Not modelled. Splash onto other parts of the same enemy, blast radius falloff, gas damage over time, burn stacking and refreshes, shield regeneration and overpenetration.
